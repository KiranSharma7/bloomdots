# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two overlapping audiences, both equally primary:

- **Brand managers and marketing leads** at mid-size companies who are evaluating agency partners for ongoing community, cultural, or content marketing campaigns. They want to see proof of cultural fluency, not just a portfolio grid.
- **Startup founders and small business owners** looking for growth-focused digital and content marketing to build brand awareness. They're skeptical of generic agencies and respond to specificity and confidence.

Primary action: feel compelled enough by the site itself to reach out, book a call, or start a conversation. The site is the pitch.

## Product Purpose

BloomDots is an Australian creative agency website. Its job is to make potential clients feel that BloomDots understands culture in a way their current agency does not, and that working with them would produce something that doesn't look or feel like everything else.

Tagline: "Make Your Brand Unskippable." Sub-positioning: "Community-led. Culture-smart. Performance-backed."

Services: Community Marketing, Cultural Marketing, Content Marketing, Digital Marketing.

Success is a visitor who finishes scrolling and thinks "these people get it" before they've read a single service description.

## Brand Personality

Culturally sharp, unafraid, motion-native.

Three words: **edgy, intentional, alive.**

The emotional goal is confident provocation — not aggression, not warmth, not inspiration-poster energy. The site should feel like it was made by people who consume culture seriously and translate that into brand work.

References that capture the right feel: WiredCo (oversized statements, bold contrast, project-led storytelling), Brandalism (editorial, content-structured), Portal One (premium restraint), We Are Social (culturally fluent).

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS startup templates**: soft gradients, hero-metric layouts (big number + small label), cream backgrounds, identical card grids, gradient text.
- **Typical agency portfolios**: hero images of laptops, client logo grids front-and-center, generic 3-step process diagrams, stock photography.
- **Overly maximalist / loud**: neon on every surface, competing motion on every element, chaotic visual hierarchy.
- **Corporate / enterprise cold**: navy suits, formal copy tone, stock imagery, anything that signals "safe choice."

## Design Principles

1. **The site should survive its own AI slop test.** If someone could look at any screen and say "AI made that" without hesitation, it has failed. Every layout decision is a design choice, not a template fill.
2. **Color as signal, not decoration.** Yellow, blue, and orange appear only where they carry meaning: active states, dot graphics, timeline markers, hover cues, numeric highlights. The dark base (#0b0b0d) is the canvas; accent colors earn their place.
3. **Motion is the medium.** GSAP animations are not embellishment — they are how the brand communicates energy and intentionality. Every section has one signature behavior.
4. **Brutalist structure, premium finish.** Sharp edges, editorial asymmetry, oversized type — but executed with precision and restraint. Not rough. Not loud. Deliberate.
5. **Show culture, don't explain it.** The design should demonstrate taste before any copy is read. No introductory paragraphs that re-explain the heading.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA compliance. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` — GSAP animations must have fallback states that preserve legibility when motion is reduced. Strong text contrast ratios maintained throughout. Keyboard navigation supported for all interactive elements.
